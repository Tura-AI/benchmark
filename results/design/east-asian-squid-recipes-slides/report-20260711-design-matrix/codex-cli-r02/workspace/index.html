The texture rule
Slide 02 · Before you begin

Cook it fast—or gently for a long time.

Squid is tender at the extremes and rubbery in the middle. Most recipes here use fierce heat for 1–4 minutes; braises and stuffed squid deliberately cross into longer, gentle cooking.

Fast: 60 sec–4 minStir-fry, grill, fry, steam. Dry well and preheat fully.
Long: 25–60 minBraise, simmer, bake. Moist heat relaxes connective tissue.
Score thick bodiesShallow crosshatches reduce curl and carry sauce.
Salt late for dry heatHelps prevent moisture from flooding the pan.
Cleaning baseline: pull out head and innards, remove the clear quill, cut below the eyes, push out the beak, peel skin if desired, rinse briefly, then dry extremely well.
Stir-fry · Korea
Slide 03 · Method 01

Ojingeo-bokkeum

Korean culinary tradition · spicy high-heat stir-fry
Serves 3–4
Prep 20 min
Cook 6 min

Ingredients

  • 600 g cleaned squid, scored and sliced
  • ½ onion, 1 carrot, 2 scallions
  • 2 tbsp gochujang; 1 tbsp gochugaru
  • 1½ tbsp soy sauce; 1 tbsp sugar
  • 1 tbsp rice syrup or honey
  • 2 tsp minced garlic; 1 tsp sesame oil

Steps

  1. Mix gochujang, chile flakes, soy, sugar, syrup, garlic and sesame oil.
  2. Heat a wok until smoking; add 1 tbsp neutral oil and vegetables. Fry 2 minutes.
  3. Add squid and sauce. Toss hard for 2–3 minutes, just until opaque and curled.
  4. Finish with scallions and sesame seeds; serve immediately with rice.
Keep the wok uncrowded. If the squid releases water, remove it briefly, reduce the sauce, then return for 20 seconds.

Grill · Japan
Slide 04 · Method 02

Ika-yaki

Japanese festival and izakaya tradition · whole grilled squid
Serves 2
Prep 15 min
Cook 7–9 min

Ingredients

  • 2 whole cleaned squid, 250–300 g each
  • 2 tbsp soy sauce
  • 2 tbsp mirin
  • 1 tbsp sake
  • 1 tsp sugar
  • ½ lemon; shichimi togarashi

Steps

  1. Cut shallow rings across each mantle without slicing through. Skewer lengthwise to limit curling.
  2. Simmer soy, mirin, sake and sugar for 2 minutes; reserve half.
  3. Grill over high heat 3 minutes per side, brushing repeatedly with glaze.
  4. Brush with reserved clean glaze, rest 1 minute, slice and serve with lemon and shichimi.
A very hot grate creates smoky edges before the interior toughens. Oil the squid, not the grill.

Salt & Pepper Squid

Hong Kong Cantonese restaurant tradition · crisp deep-fry
Serves 4
Prep 25 min
Cook 10 min

Ingredients

  • 600 g squid tubes and tentacles
  • 80 g cornstarch; 40 g plain flour
  • 1 tsp fine salt; 1 tsp white pepper
  • ½ tsp five-spice powder
  • 4 garlic cloves, sliced
  • 2 red chiles; 3 scallions; frying oil

Steps

  1. Dry squid thoroughly. Mix starch, flour, half the salt, white pepper and five-spice.
  2. Heat oil to 185°C / 365°F. Coat squid immediately before frying.
  3. Fry in small batches for 60–90 seconds; drain on a rack.
  4. In 1 tbsp oil, fry garlic, chile and scallion 30 seconds. Return squid, toss with remaining seasoning and serve.
Cornstarch gives the brittle shell; flour adds enough structure to keep it attached.

Clay-pot reduction · Taiwan
Slide 06 · Method 04

Three-Cup Squid

Taiwanese sanbeiji tradition · sesame-soy-rice wine reduction
Serves 3
Prep 15 min
Cook 9 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g squid, scored in bite-size pieces
  • 1 tbsp toasted sesame oil + 1 tbsp neutral oil
  • 20 g ginger, sliced; 6 garlic cloves
  • 3 tbsp light soy sauce
  • 3 tbsp Taiwanese rice wine
  • 1 tbsp rock sugar; 1 packed cup Thai basil

Steps

  1. Blanch squid 20 seconds; drain very well.
  2. Heat oils in a clay pot or wok. Fry ginger 2 minutes; add garlic and chile.
  3. Add soy, wine and sugar; boil 2–3 minutes until glossy.
  4. Add squid and toss 60–90 seconds. Turn off heat, fold in basil, cover 30 seconds.
The “three cups” are a flavor ratio, not literal modern cup measures. Reduce first; add squid last.

Stuff & grill · Thailand

Pla Muek Yang Sai

Thai street-food tradition · stuffed charcoal-grilled squid
Serves 4
Prep 30 min
Cook 12 min

Ingredients

  • 4 cleaned squid, about 180 g each
  • 250 g ground pork
  • 2 tsp fish sauce; 1 tsp soy sauce
  • 1 tsp sugar; ½ tsp white pepper
  • 2 garlic cloves; 2 tbsp chopped cilantro stems
  • Sauce: 2 tbsp lime, 1½ tbsp fish sauce, chile, garlic, 1 tsp sugar

Steps

  1. Mix pork, fish sauce, soy, sugar, pepper, garlic and cilantro stems.
  2. Fill mantles only ⅔ full; secure with toothpicks and score lightly.
  3. Grill over medium-high charcoal, turning often, 10–12 minutes until filling reaches 71°C / 160°F.
  4. Rest 3 minutes, slice, and spoon over the sharp green seafood sauce.
Do not pack tightly: the squid contracts while the filling expands.

Mực Hấp Sả

Vietnamese coastal tradition · lemongrass-chile steam
Serves 3–4
Prep 15 min
Cook 6–8 min

Ingredients

  • 600 g small whole squid or thick rings
  • 3 lemongrass stalks, bruised and cut
  • 20 g ginger, julienned
  • 2 bird’s-eye chiles, sliced
  • 2 scallions; 1 tbsp fish sauce
  • Dip: 2 tbsp lime, 1½ tbsp fish sauce, 1 tbsp sugar, chile

Steps

  1. Toss squid with fish sauce for 5 minutes.
  2. Line a heatproof plate with lemongrass; arrange squid, ginger, chile and scallion over it.
  3. Steam over rapidly boiling water: rings 4–5 minutes, small whole squid 6–8 minutes.
  4. Drain accumulated liquid and serve immediately with lime-fish sauce dip and herbs.
Steam is gentle but unforgiving: stop as soon as the flesh turns opaque and springs back.

Braise · Philippines

Adobong Pusit

Filipino adobo tradition · vinegar-soy squid braise
Serves 4
Prep 20 min
Cook 25 min

Ingredients

  • 800 g small squid, ink sacs reserved if available
  • 1 onion, sliced; 5 garlic cloves
  • 2 tomatoes, diced
  • 80 ml cane vinegar
  • 45 ml soy sauce; 120 ml water
  • 2 bay leaves; 1 tsp peppercorns; 1 tsp brown sugar

Steps

  1. Sauté onion, garlic and tomatoes in 1 tbsp oil for 5 minutes.
  2. Add squid, vinegar, soy, water, bay, peppercorns and ink. Do not stir until boiling.
  3. Simmer uncovered 3 minutes; remove squid to prevent toughness.
  4. Reduce sauce 12–15 minutes. Return squid for 2 minutes; balance with sugar.
This split cooking protects the squid while preserving the deeply reduced adobo character.

Macanese Curry-Baked Squid

Macanese Luso-Asian tradition · coconut curry gratin
Serves 4
Prep 25 min
Bake 18 min

Ingredients

  • 600 g squid rings, dried well
  • 1 onion; 2 garlic cloves
  • 1½ tbsp mild curry powder
  • 200 ml coconut milk; 100 ml stock
  • 1 tsp turmeric; 1 tsp sugar; 1 tbsp fish sauce
  • 250 g parboiled potato cubes; 30 g breadcrumbs

Steps

  1. Sauté onion and garlic 5 minutes. Bloom curry and turmeric 30 seconds.
  2. Add coconut milk, stock, sugar and fish sauce; reduce 6 minutes. Fold in potatoes.
  3. Flash-sear squid 45 seconds in a separate hot pan, then place in a baking dish with sauce.
  4. Top with crumbs; bake at 220°C / 425°F for 8–10 minutes until bubbling.
Macanese cooking layers Portuguese baking with South and Southeast Asian spice routes. The flash-sear keeps the bake brief.

Ojingeo-guk

Korean home-cooking tradition · spicy radish squid soup
Serves 4
Prep 15 min
Cook 22 min

Ingredients

  • 450 g squid, sliced
  • 300 g Korean radish, thin rectangles
  • 1.2 L anchovy-kelp stock
  • 1½ tbsp gochugaru
  • 1 tbsp soup soy sauce; 2 tsp fish sauce
  • 2 garlic cloves; 2 scallions; 1 green chile

Steps

  1. Simmer radish in stock for 12–15 minutes until nearly tender.
  2. Add gochugaru, soup soy, fish sauce and garlic; simmer 3 minutes.
  3. Add squid and chile; cook only 2–3 minutes.
  4. Adjust salt, add scallions, and serve steaming with rice and kimchi.
Build the broth and soften the radish first. Squid is the final ingredient, not the soup base.

Stuff & simmer · Japan
Slide 12 · Method 10

Ikameshi

Hokkaido Japanese tradition · rice-stuffed simmered squid
Serves 4
Soak 1 hr
Cook 45–55 min

Ingredients

  • 4 small whole cleaned squid, 150–180 g each
  • 120 g glutinous rice, soaked 1 hour
  • 500 ml dashi or water
  • 60 ml soy sauce; 60 ml sake
  • 45 ml mirin; 25 g sugar
  • 10 g ginger, sliced

Steps

  1. Drain rice. Fill squid mantles just ⅓ full and close with toothpicks.
  2. Bring dashi, soy, sake, mirin, sugar and ginger to a simmer.
  3. Add squid, cover with a drop lid, and simmer gently 40–50 minutes, turning twice.
  4. Rest in liquid 10 minutes. Slice into thick rounds and glaze with reduced broth.
The low fill level is essential: glutinous rice expands dramatically and can split the mantle.

Slide 14 · Kitchen field notes

Shopping, thawing & prep

Buy: clear eyes, clean ocean smell, glossy flesh; frozen is often excellent.
Size: small whole squid for stuffing; large tubes for scoring and frying.
Thaw: overnight in the refrigerator, then drain and dry on towels.
Yield: expect 65–75% usable weight after cleaning whole squid.
Ink: reserve intact sacs for adobo, pasta, rice, or sauce.
Tentacles: cook them too; trim below the eyes and remove the hard beak.
Crosshatch: score the inside surface so pieces curl outward attractively.
Food safety: keep raw seafood below 4°C / 40°F and avoid cross-contamination.

The final five
Slide 15 · Cook with confidence

Five principles to remember

Dry squid aggressively before grilling, frying, or stir-frying.
Use the hottest pan you can safely manage for quick methods.
Build sauces and broths before adding squid.
For stuffing, leave room for both filling expansion and mantle contraction.
Taste the tradition: preserve each dish’s defining acid, chile, herb, or fermented seasoning.

Start with the technique that matches your time: salt-and-pepper squid for speed, ika-yaki for fire, adobong pusit for depth, or ikameshi for a slow weekend project.
